## Independent OOS findings

### 1. Early Response Breadth must be early, not eventual
The five-day sequence deteriorated sharply: daily mean MFE ~9.71%, 8.29%, 4.81%, 3.66%, 2.12%, while TP2 breadth was 1/9, 0/10, 1/9, 0/10, 0/10 among executed trades. High fill breadth did not imply fast-detach breadth. This independently supports the prior correction: fill count and end-of-window mean MFE are not valid proxies for Early Response Breadth.

Candidate refinement for future OOS only: define Early Response Breadth from a bounded post-EDP clock using rapid Time-to-Positive, low pre-expansion MAE, and early +5/+8 response. The current v1 frozen schema cannot compute this literally.

### 2. Snapshot PE remains a discovery variable, not EAP authorization
07-02 BROCCOLI714 had PE6/12/24/72 ~1/.865/.409/.104 but only +3.05% MFE over ~8.5h. SONIC had ~1/.720/.388/.082, low MAE ~-0.77%, but only +6.13% MFE over ~41.75h. SCR had strong ~.971/.869/.592/.178 and similarly low MAE ~-0.77%, yet only +6.15% MFE over ~43h. These are clean stale-response examples.

07-03 OPEN had PE6 ~1 but MFE only +0.09% and stopped after ~0.25h. HANA had PE6 ~1 but MFE only +0.95% over ~13h. These continue the E3-analogue evidence: a short-horizon efficiency extreme without actual price hand-off/acceptance is not sufficient.

### 3. Strong eventual MFE can still be low capital efficiency
07-01 XAN reached ~+19.92% recorded MFE but held ~15h; XNY reached ~+17.49% over ~9.25h; RESOLV ~+17.90% over ~28h. These are useful upside paths but not equivalent to <=3h/6h Fast Detach.

### 4. Catastrophic-path veto survives another OOS challenge
07-01 BASED produced MFE ~+6.15% but MAE ~-15.48%, `LiqRiskFlag=true`, and stopped after ~0.5h. Any Fast Detach score that rewards its eventual upside without a hard adverse-path veto is invalid for the stated capital-efficiency objective.

### 5. Weak breadth should expire old signals, not merely down-rank them
07-03 to 07-05 daily mean MFE decayed ~4.81% -> 3.66% -> 2.12%, with only one TP2 on 07-03 and none thereafter. Combined with stale individual paths, this supports the prior candidate that weak early-response breadth should accelerate Stale Response Decay. A later market recovery, if observed, should reset the clock only for fresh signals; expired ignition should not be resurrected without a new EDP/EAP event.
